## What is transmitted without ever being completely understood

### Foreword
This novel is a reflection on what remains when men disappear.

The facts disappear.
The witnesses die.
Memories transform.
The secrets are poorly understood.
And the generations reconstruct what they have not experienced.

Time keeps nothing intact.

It transforms faces, shifts memories, fixes silences and gives children wounds whose origins they sometimes ignore.

A child sees a kiss. He doesn't know the lovers' story. Their jealousies. Their silences.

He doesn't get their story. He receives his perception from what he has seen.

This perception generates a new wave, which creates a new perception.

Fears create secrets.
Secrets shape behavior.
These behaviors in turn become perceptions for those who experience them.

And so on.

A story can show the source of the wave. Another can show its propagation.

Men never convey what they wanted to say. They transmit what others thought they understood.

To write is to try to make the path of the wave visible.

Writing is not just about transmitting what happened. It’s trying to convey what was felt.

And this thing, despite the years, despite the deaths, despite the lies and the forgettings, can go through time without completely losing its form.

Two stories can then seem separate. They may even have been written by different men, in different generations.

However, they may only be two ends of the same wave.

That of an injury that is transmitted.
That of a love that remains.
That of a story written from the heart.


### 4th cover

The Wave
What is transmitted without ever being completely understood

It all starts with a kiss.
A child sees it.
Years later, someone dies leaving behind a secret they were never able to tell.
Then a book is discovered in a chest. There is talk of a treasure.

From a repatriated cassette.
From an expedition of 1837.
Some claim that the deposit did not come from 1837.
That it would have already changed hands the first time during the Seven Years' War.
Others whisper the name of Pondicherry.
Nobody knows anything about it.

What seemed like one story becomes a fragment of another.
What belonged to the past begins to act again in the present.
And behind the characters, behind the generations, behind the silences, the same story stubbornly seeks to go back to its source.

Two novels. Two eras. A single wave.

### Author note
Charles-Philip Eldon

That's not my real name.
I am nobody. I am everyone.

We are born, we grow, we act. We receive a story as an inheritance, then we add pieces to it. Some are ours. Others were already there when we arrived.

I am the youngest son of La Charpente.
I would have been the priest of the Post Office.
I was born in 2000.

I should have been a girl.
I am what I am.
I look at the past and its mysteries make me smile. Maybe because they are no longer completely mine. Maybe because they still are.

But I don't have time anymore.
I need to renovate a childhood home.
